Train simulation (rail)

Reference of rail, a simple simulation of a train along a route with a landscape (16:9) video, in the style of the traffic video. The example is the Chepe from Los Mochis to Chihuahua through the Copper Canyon (~653 km). The code is a separate package, src/rail/, that does not depend on the traffic package. The traffic simulation is documented in config.html.

Usage Model Route file Realistic mode Options Outputs

Usage

uv run rail                                   # examples/route_chepe.toml → results/<ID>_chepe/
uv run rail --realistic                       # real track + slow motion through the canyons → results/<ID>_chepe_realistic/
uv run rail --route chihuahua-mochis           # the same trip in the opposite direction → results/<ID>_chepe_chihuahua-mochis/
uv run rail my_route.toml --name test
uv run rail --duration 90 --format mp4 --depart 07:30
uv run rail --no-video                        # timetable, summary and route.png only

The route does not use config.toml: it is described in its own TOML file, by default examples/route_chepe.toml. Without --realistic everything works offline and the result is the usual one.

Model

One-dimensional kinematics, with a fixed step of dt_s.

The train accelerates at accel_ms2 up to the segment limit and brakes at brake_ms2 in time for the next lower limit and to stop at each station. At each station it waits dwell_min. The position on the map and the elevation are interpolated linearly by km between the route points. It does not model gradients, power or tunnels: the speed per segment sums all that up. With the limits of the example, Los Mochis → Creel takes ~9 h 40 min, the published time of the Chepe Express.

Approximate data. The km are derived from the official kilometre posts, which are counted from Ojinaga (km = 921 − official km). The coordinates are those of each town or, where it exists, those of the station in OpenStreetMap (Sufragio, Loreto, Pitorreal). The elevations of the example are the terrain elevations from SRTM at each point of the real track, smoothed (see Realistic mode). Without --realistic, the map line joins those points with straight lines and the profile is interpolated between them.

Realistic mode: --realistic

Uses the real track geometry, taken from OpenStreetMap, and plays the Copper Canyon in slow motion.

  • Real track. The first time, the relation osm_relation (for the Chepe, 5977762 «Chihuahua al Pacífico») is downloaded from the Overpass API and saved in track_file (examples/track_chepe.csv). It retries on a mirror server if the main one is busy. Later runs read the CSV and need no internet. To download it again, delete the CSV.
  • Points on the track. Each station, landmark and shape point is projected onto the track, and its km becomes the real distance along it. For the Chepe, the track is 653.4 km long from Los Mochis to Chihuahua (official: 653 km). The speed limits still come from the route file.
  • Real elevation. With elevation_file, the first time the track is sampled every elevation_step_km (655 points for the Chepe) and their elevation is requested from the public OpenTopoData API (90 m SRTM terrain model), in batches of 100 points and one request per second. It is saved in examples/elevation_chepe.csv and later runs need no internet. SRTM measures the terrain, not the track: under a bridge it gives the bottom of the canyon and over a tunnel the top of the hill. That is why the profile is smoothed with a 5 km running median, and each station and landmark takes the profile's elevation at its km. Compared with published values, the differences are a few tens of metres (Divisadero 2246 m against 2240 m; Creel 2349 m against ~2340 m). If the track is downloaded again, this CSV must be deleted too; if it does not match the track, the program warns.
  • Zoom. In the upper-left corner of the map a box shows the section slow_from – slow_to (Témoris – Creel) with the train moving through it. A dashed rectangle marks that section on the map, and the profile shades it.
  • Slow motion. In that section the video runs slow_factor times slower (×3 for the Chepe) and shows «slow motion ×3» at the top right. The video lasts 90 s by default instead of 60 s, so that the rest of the trip does not go by too fast.

Track data: © OpenStreetMap contributors, under the ODbL licence. Elevation: SRTM (NASA), via OpenTopoData. The video and route.png carry this attribution.

Outputs

They are saved in results/<date-time>_<name>/. Nothing is ever overwritten:

  • summary.txt: distance, departure and arrival times, total time, time dwelling at stops and mean speed. If the route goes through Los Mochis and Creel, the time between them (in the direction of travel) to compare it with the Chepe Express. With --realistic, also the source of the track and the elevation, and the slow-motion section.
  • timetable.csv: one row per station with km, elevation, arrival, departure and dwell. If it arrives after midnight, the time carries the suffix (+1d).
  • route.png: the map, the elevation profile and the list of points of interest with their km, in the colours of --style (the video uses them too).
  • journey.webm (or .mp4/.gif), 1280×720: on the left, the map with the travelled section and the train (blue and red in the default style); on the right, the clock, the km, the speed, the elevation and the next stop. The clock is the time of day on the trip (departure from --depart plus the simulated time, including stops); below it the departure time and the elapsed time are shown (e.g. «departure 06:00 · 11h 35min elapsed»). When passing a landmark or a station its name and note appear, and a ring highlights it on the map. At the bottom right is the elevation profile with the train's position.
